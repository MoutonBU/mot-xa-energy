# MCP Architecture and Interiors XA Energy - Release notes

Current version: **1.86.221**
(`MCP_XA_Energy_Setup_x.y.z.exe`) and in Windows' list of installed programs.

## What is new since the first version (1.0.0)

### New features
- **Each tool's colour runs through the whole tool:** open Costing and the buttons, table header lines, selected ribbon buttons, side-bar highlight and the icons' hover tint all turn purple; Invoicing goes green, Orders amber, Detail tool blue, Drawing register teal, Site brown. The start screen and the SANS tools keep the program's red.
- **Clearer message when an update fails:** the box now says where in the program it failed and whether the update may still have been installed, and the details are written to the update log (mot_update.log in the Temp folder).
- **Start screen shows your recent projects:** under the tiles, a "Jump back in" row of cards for your last four projects, newest first, each with its name, when it was last edited and its folder. Click a card to open it. "New project" and "Open a project..." buttons sit beside it.
- **The sewer follows the ground fall you turned:** when you set the fall direction yourself (and there are no contours), the ground level at the other end of the sewer is worked out from how far the pipe runs along that direction, so the depth, drops and long section change with it. With contours the real ground is still used.
- **Turn the ground fall where you want it:** three new buttons in the plan look options: "Show which way the ground falls" (click the downhill direction), "Turn the fall 15°" and "Fall: automatic". The arrows, label and water streaks follow your direction and keep the steepness from the contours or typed levels.
- **Ground-fall arrows also work without contours:** if there is no contour file, the arrows, label and water streaks use the two ground levels you typed in the sewer section (at the house and at the main). The label then says "from the typed levels". With only one level typed there is nothing to show.
- **Which way the ground falls (with contours):** small blue arrows on the stand point downhill, with a label "GROUND FALLS 1:20 (5.0 %)" beside the stand; on the screen, thin blue water streaks run downhill over the stand; a tick box in the plan look options turns the arrows and streaks off. The sewer checks gain a pill "Ground" that says how the ground falls along the sewer towards the main and whether that makes the pipe deeper or shallower at the main.
- **Several drops in the sewer:** a new "Number of drops" box (1 to 5) next to the drop choice. With "the program places it" the drops are spread along the pipe (on an eye where one is near); with "I choose where" each drop has its own box for its place. All the drops are the same height and the pipe still arrives exactly at the main's invert level. Each drop gets its own rodding eye and its own steep part on the long section, the plan, the table and the elevation. If you put two drops too close together the program moves them apart and says so.
- **Levels can be below zero:** the ground level at the house, the ground level at the main, the main sewer invert level and the finished floor level now accept negative values (for example -2.500 m). "Not set" is now the very bottom of the box, so 0.000 is a real level. Older projects open as before (a 0 in an old project still means "not set"). The "No contours yet" card text is no longer cut off.
- **Every tab has its own icon and colour (muted, on the same dark look):** the start tiles, the left bar items and the numbered step buttons inside each tool (for example "1 Stand and house") each have their own icon and their own soft colour instead of all being red; Invoicing, Costing, Guidelines review and Building lines no longer share an icon with another tool. The "No contours yet" card is no longer cut off.
- **Site plan: setbacks, ground, dimensions, ruler (screen; the ground colours also on the PDF, not the DXF):** while a house or carport is selected the setback lines glow amber when it is close and red when it is over; when you put it down it settles with a shadow and a small bounce and the setback lines flash green or red; its distances to the boundaries slide out as dimension lines and fold away again; with contours imported the stand is coloured by height (blue low, brown high) with a red glow where it is steeper than 1 in 10 (a tick box turns it off); hold R and a ruler follows the pointer with the distance and angle; stretches of sewer with too little soil cover flash red on the plan and are a red band marked "cover too low" on the long section.
- **More movement on the site plan (screen only, never the PDF):** blue dots run along the water line to the house, amber sparks along the electrical line, a soft ring pulses out of every eye and every connection point one after the other, and the outline of a selected house, carport, sewer or eye has dashes running round it. One tick box under the plan look options turns all the movement off.
- **Rounder controls, tool colours, flow and selection:** tick boxes, round buttons, sliders, progress bars and entry boxes share the same rounded look; the folded sections on Site services have an icon and a one-line summary while closed (for example "110 mm pipe, fall 1:50, 450 mm deep at the house"); each tool has its own colour on the left bar (a stripe at the top and the selected item); small brown dots drift along the sewer from the house to the main on the screen (a tick box under the look options turns it off; never on the PDF); when you click the house, carport, sewer or an eye it gets a soft red outline and the rest of the plan dims a little. The plan is now shown from a stored picture while the dots move, so it stays light.
- **More polish:** messages ("PDF saved", "Contours imported") slide in at the corner as small cards; the sewer checks show as green and red pills above the buttons; the section preview shows the sheet as paper on a dark desk with a shadow and corner marks (Fit the width / Full size buttons); plan zoom glides; empty screens (no contours, no saved details) show a friendly card with a big button; icon line weight is now matched to the icon size; darker theme has clearer borders, headers and disabled text; a splash with the logo and a red line shows while the program opens; hold Ctrl a moment and Ctrl+1... shows on the left bar buttons. (The extra red glow I added last time is removed: buttons already had one.)
- **Visual polish:** pages fade in when you switch tabs; buttons get a soft red glow when pointed at (tighter when pressed, and when tabbed to); the grey tool tips are now small cards with the icon, the name and what it does; the light/dark switch fades instead of flashing; a thin red bar with a spinner shows while a PDF, DXF or contour import is made; the folded sections on the Site services screen are soft rounded cards. Imported contours are drawn faintly on the site plan with their levels, and the long section has the same "Sewer as brown lines only" tick box as the elevation.
- **Icons no longer jiggle, and every icon turns red:** the wobble on the big tile icons and the bounce on the small button icons are gone; they now just grow smoothly. Every button icon (top icons, bar, all other buttons) turns red while you point at it or press it, and goes back to its own colour afterwards.
- **A drop has a rodding eye only:** the program no longer draws an inspection eye at a drop. The rodding eye at the top of the steep part is what is needed to rod down it (SANS 10252-2, 6.5.2.2). An eye that is already on the pipe at that spot stays. The long section, the site plan, the table, the checks and the sewer elevation all follow. (Whether your council wants more access there is for you to confirm.)
- **Left bar icons turn red** while you point at them, and grow a little (they are sharper now when they grow).
- **Detail generator learns your details:** after a detail is drawn, "Save as my detail" keeps it. The next time you ask for a detail the generator is shown your saved details that are closest to the request (up to two) as examples, so new details are drawn the way you draw them: your material names, layers, note wording. "My details" lists them; "Open my detail" puts one back in the workspace and "Delete it" removes it. They are kept in the Detail notes outputs folder (my_details.json). Tested with a pretend reply only, because no API key was available here.
- **Detail notes is less cramped:** the new "Hide the notes table" button gives the drawing the whole width, and the table needs less width when it is shown.
- **Animated icons:** on the start screens the big tile icons now come alive: pointing at a tile lifts the icon, grows it, wobbles it once and lights a soft red glow behind it; pressing squeezes it and sends a ring spreading out, then it pops back. The small icons on every button in the program (Preview, Save, Add ...) grow a little while you point at them, squeeze when pressed and settle back, without changing the size of the button. Buttons without an icon are left alone. (The icons in the left bar's list are not animated yet.)
- **Contoured sites:** in the sewer section settings, "Import contours (DXF)..." reads the contours of a site plan or survey (polylines with an elevation or Z, flat polylines with the level written on them, and spot heights; millimetre drawings are understood). The contours must be in the same coordinates as the stand. The ground along the sewer is then read off the contours every half metre (between the two nearest contour levels), so the long section, the eyes' ground levels, the covers and the sewer elevation follow the real ground. The cover is checked every half metre: if the ground falls faster than the pipe and the pipe would come out of the ground, the check names the chainage where it happens and suggests starting deeper or using a steeper fall. "Take the ground levels from the contours" switches them off again (the typed levels are used) and "Remove the contours" deletes them. Tested on made-up contours only: check it on a real survey.
- **Sewer elevation: pipes or brown lines:** the tick box "Sewer as brown lines only (no pipe width)" draws the sewer as one brown line at the invert level, with single lines for the eyes, instead of the pipes with their outside diameter.
- **Drops are easier to set:** the "Drops to the main (distance = invert level)" box is gone. In the sewer section settings choose "Drop to reach the main": No drop / the program places it / I choose where. For "I choose where" type one distance: metres along the sewer from the house outlet (0 m is the first rodding eye at the house; the main sewer is at the far end). The program works out the invert level after the drop itself so the pipe arrives exactly at the main, and a line of text under the choices says in words where the drop is, how deep it is and how far it is before the main (or that the pipe arrives above or below the main). Projects saved with a typed drop open as "I choose where" at that distance.
- **Sewer long section in the same look as the coverage report:** a grey rounded header panel with the MCP logo, the title and the scales, the red bar, a rounded information card (stand, architect, revision, sheet, date) and rounded boxes for the table, the checks and the notes, on the A1 sheet. The graph is taller. The logo is on the PDF and the picture; the DXF has the same boxes without the logo. Check the sheet on your own site.
- **Long section spread out, and a true 45 degree drop:** the section uses more of the A1 sheet (taller graph, scales 1:50, 1:75, 1:100, 1:150 ... for the length) and the table moved down so nothing is cramped. The drop is now exactly 45 degrees on the printed sheet, in line with the rodding eye at the drop, so the eye and the drop read as one diagonal from the ground down to the lower pipe. The program works the length of that diagonal out from the sheet's scales, and "Add a drop to reach the main" adjusts the lower level until the pipe arrives exactly at the main. The "drop N mm" label is beside the diagonal. Check it on your own site.
- **Sewer long section on an A1 sheet:** the PDF is now A1 landscape (the DXF is drawn at the same size) and the horizontal scale is chosen from 1:50, 1:100, 1:200 ... so a normal stand gets 1:100; the sheet states the real printed scales. The eye names are spread over four heights and the "NATURAL GROUND LEVEL" label sits beside the end of the ground line, so less overlaps. Check the sheet on your own site.
- **The program now places the drop itself:** the pipe runs from the minimum invert (450 mm) at its fall, drops steeply at an eye, and the lower pipe runs on at the same fall and arrives exactly at the main's invert. "Add a drop to reach the main" works out the drop height and puts the drop on the last eye that still leaves the lower pipe a run of its own (no question asked; you can still type or change the drop in the settings box). The steep part is drawn at 45 degrees on the sheet and takes half its height in metres of chainage. Check the placement on your own site.
- **Drop drawn as in your blue sketch:** on the long section the drop is now one straight 45 degree line, the rodding eye's line carried on from the upper pipe down to the lower pipe, which then carries on at the fall to the main. Check it against your sketch on your own site.
- **Drops now show on the site plan, and lean like your sketch:** every drop typed in the sewer section settings puts an inspection eye and a rodding eye on the sewer line of the site plan at that distance, labelled with the same names as the long section (IE1, RE1 ...). On the long section the drop leans at 45 degrees up toward the house, like a rodding eye, and the pipe carries on lower to the main. "Add a drop to reach the main" now asks where the pipe drops. Tested on the test site; check the positions on your own site. I took "gut placement" to mean the sewer line on the site plan.
- **Drops to get the pipe into the main:** in the sewer section settings, "Drops to the main (distance = invert level after the drop)", for example "8.6 = -0.477", and the button "Add a drop to reach the main", which puts a drop at the last eye before the main, deep enough for the pipe to arrive at the main's invert level. At each drop the program adds an inspection eye and a rodding eye, steps the pipe down inside the eye and carries on at the fall. The long section draws the drop and lists the eyes in the table; the sewer elevation sheet uses the same drops. Eyes now carry the same names on both sheets (RE1, IE1, RE2 ...): the elevation labels each eye with its name and the CL / IL / D boxes show it (two eyes at one place share a box, e.g. IE2 / RE2); an extra inspection eye the elevation adds under a stack that is not on the site plan is numbered on from the plan's own. Tested on the test site and a simulated plan; check the drop and eye positions on your own site.
- **Sewer elevation now comes from screenshots only (the IFC route is removed):** in the sewer elevation settings, "Add a floor plan screenshot (AI)" (one per floor: Claude reads where the WCs, basins, showers, baths, sinks and washing machines are) and "Add an elevation screenshot (AI)" (Claude reads the floor levels written on it, such as +218 600 Ground Floor, and the floor plans then take their levels from it). The plan is laid onto the house on the site plan using the stand's orientation and the sheet is drawn as before; "Turn the plan" and "View from the other side" fix a wrong facing. Checked with simulated AI replies only (no live request run here), so look over what it read before trusting the sheet: fixtures, floor names and levels.
- **Detail generator has the whole Detail notes workspace:** the generator tab is now a request box on top and, below it, a full Detail notes workspace (click a part to choose its spec, the catalog and My specs, templates, auto note, brickwork and colours, the notes schedule, outputs). The detail Claude draws is opened in it with Claude's notes already in the list, so you can change any note from the catalog or add the rest with Auto note. The separate Save DXF and Add notes buttons are gone: save from the workspace as in Detail notes.
- **Sewer elevation from floor plan screenshots (no IFC needed):** next to "Choose the IFC model", "Or add a floor plan screenshot (AI)" takes a screenshot of one floor plan, asks which floor it is and its level, and Claude (using your API key from Settings) lists the WCs, basins, showers, baths, sinks and washing machines and where they are. Add one screenshot per floor. The plan is scaled so the building outline on the screenshot is as long as the house on the site plan, and then the same turning, stacks, levels and sheet as the IFC route are used. The positions come from an AI reading of a picture: check that the fixtures on the sheet are where they are on the plan (an unclear screenshot gives wrong or missing fixtures). Tested with a simulated reply and a blank picture only; no live request has been run (no key in the build environment).
- **Rodding eyes drawn at 45 degrees:** on the sewer long section and the sewer elevation every rodding eye (RE) now leans at 45 degrees up the line, back towards the house, instead of standing straight up. Inspection eyes (IE) stay vertical. The direction is my reading of your marked-up drawing: if you want them to lean the other way, say so.
- **Detail generator: "tool_choice ... not supported for this model" fixed:** the request no longer forces the drawing tool, which some models refuse, and still reads the answer whether it comes back as the tool call or as plain text. Not run against the live service here (no key in the build environment).
- **Detail generator no longer fails with "The reply could not be read":** the request now asks Claude for the drawing as a checked structure instead of free text, so a stray comma can no longer break it, and an unreadable reply is tried once more. Retaining walls and proprietary blocks (Loffelstein and similar) are drawn as generic shapes with "to manufacturer's spec" in the note and the assumed sizes listed. Checked against a simulated reply only: no Anthropic key in the build environment, so a live request has not been run.
- **House placing, sewer elevation from the IFC model:** under the sewer settings, "Sewer elevation from the IFC model". Choose the building's IFC model and the program reads the WCs, basins, showers, baths, sinks and washing machines (found by their name or type) and the storeys, lays the model's outline over the house on the site plan (it takes the way round that puts the wet fixtures nearest the sewer; "Turn the model" tries the other fits and "View from the other side" flips the view), and draws the sewer elevation on A3 like your examples: storey levels, fixture letters at their floor, the stacks with vent pipes, the sewer along the wall with rodding and inspection eyes, the manhole, and CL / IL / D tags. Each stack's fixtures set the highest invert allowed under it (1:40 branch fall, clear of the slab), the invert at the house is the shallowest the site settings allow or deeper if a stack needs it, and the sheet checks that and the cover and the drop to the main. Save as PDF or as DXF (drawn full size at the scale printed on the sheet). Fixture outlet heights are assumed (WC 150, basins and sinks 550, washing machine 900 mm above the floor), and without typed levels the ground is assumed 150 mm under the floor and the sheet says so. Tested on a model made for the test, not on one of your Archicad IFC files: how your fixtures are named in the export, and which way the model faces, need checking on a real one.
- **Detail tool, with a new Detail generator tab:** the Detail Notes tab and home tile are now called "Detail tool". Inside it, "Detail notes" is the existing tool and "Detail generator" is new: type what you want (or press Dictate to use Windows voice typing, Windows key + H), and Claude draws the detail as a section in true size with brickwork in red with 85 mm bricks and 10 mm joints, concrete in green, the other house colours and a column of callout notes with SANS references. Save it as a DXF for ArchiCAD or send it to Detail notes to add or change notes. It needs an Anthropic API key saved under Settings (a Claude Pro subscription does not give the program one; use is billed per request, a few cents a detail). "Show a sample" draws a cavity wall on a strip footing without a key. The sizes and notes come from Claude and must be checked against SANS and your Engineer before issuing; the checks on a real request were not run here (no key in the build environment), only the drawing and note placement from a sample.
- **House placing, sewer long section for the council:** under the sewer settings, "Sewer section for the council (levels, PDF, DXF)". Type the ground level at the house and at the main (plus optional spot levels along the run, the main's invert level and the finished floor level) and the program draws an A3 sheet from the sewer you placed: natural ground line, invert and crown of the pipe at its fall, inspection and rodding eyes, the main with its invert level (or an arrow when it is far below), a table of every eye with chainage, ground level, invert level, depth, cover and fall, a checks list (fall against SANS 10252-2 table 13, soil cover, the pipe arriving above the main's invert, eye spacing), notes and a title block from the project details. Save it as PDF or as DXF (1:200 sheet, own layers, for ArchiCAD). Horizontal and vertical scales are chosen to fit and printed on the sheet. Without typed levels the sheet says the levels are assumed (ground at the house taken as 100.000); the levels must be confirmed by the land surveyor before it is submitted.
- **Light and ventilation, checked against the SANS 10400-O:2011 (Edition 3) text:** the 10 % / 5 % light, 5 % ventilation, 0,2 m2 and 0,1 m2 minimums all match clauses 4.2.1.1.3 and 4.3.1.1.3. One fix: the 0,2 m2 light minimum belongs to the 10 % rule only, so the 5 % category 1 rule no longer has it (it was making very small category 1 rooms need slightly more light than the standard asks). Edition 4 (2025) numbers are still unread, so the rules stay on Edition 3.
- **Look and fit, whole-program pass:** Building lines and House placing no longer scroll sideways (their button and number rows now wrap), the Costing fixtures page scrolls instead of squashing its tables, the schedule columns and rows are wide and tall enough to read, and the "lines have no product yet" warning has its own line instead of being cut off. The cost-by-level list now runs Basement, Ground, First, Second ... Roof instead of alphabetically. Buttons, group boxes, table headers and drop-downs have red accents and hover highlights in both the dark and the light theme. Checked against the published SANS 10400-XA:2021 text: Table 3 (shading) and Table 4 (fenestration) match the built-in numbers. SANS 10400-O Edition 4 (2025) is gazetted but its numbers are still not checked, so the rules stay on Edition 3.
- **Look and tidy-up:** number boxes now have proper up / down arrows in the dark and light themes, the Windows and Doors tables keep the Type and Opening style boxes wide enough to read (they scroll sideways instead of squeezing), and the House placing toolbar is shorter, with the less-used tools under "Tools" and "Show" menus.
- **House placing, snap to the building lines (and past them):** while you drag the house or carport, a wall within about 10 pixels of a building line (the boundary pulled in by its street, side or rear space) sits exactly on it, a corner snaps to two lines at once, and the hint line says which. Hold Alt to move freely, for instance past the line when you will ask for a relaxation; the tick "Snap the house to the building lines" in the zoning box turns the snap off altogether. Nothing ever stops the house at a line. When a line is crossed, the compliance list says how many metres over it is and that a relaxation is needed.
- **Open items before submission:** a new "Open items before submission..." under Reports lists what still stops a submission, from every tool: empty project details, light and ventilation not run or rooms failing, no compliance route, rational design not run or run only in the uncertified built-in model, no weather or site location, house placing warnings and zoning limits not met or not typed in, missing stand number, and the SANS 10400-O edition the rules come from. Items that must be fixed are red, items to look at are amber.
- **House placing, moving the house is much faster:** the house and carport now follow the mouse at once instead of waiting for you to pause, and the sewer, water and electrical lines are worked out about seven times faster while you drag (coarser grid, no side-to-side port tries, and the grid costs are kept between the three lines). The exact lines are drawn when you let go, as before. On the Blue Hills stand 2093 a drag step went from about 120 ms to about 35 ms.
- **Rational design, clause 4.2 route (Tables 1 and 2):** a new button "Tables 1 and 2 (clause 4.2)..." in the simulation window. Type in the two limits for your occupancy and energy zone from your copy of SANS 10400-XA:2021 (the tables are SABS copyright, so they are not built in) and the result page adds a pass / fail line: yearly energy against Table 1 and demand against Table 2, with no reference building. It warns when the occupancy has no data in the tables (A1 to A4, E2, E3, F1 to F3, G1, H1 to H5 do). The limits are saved with the project.
- **Light and ventilation, "Rules..." button:** the percentages and least areas of SANS 10400-O can now be edited, with the edition they come from named (SANS 10400-O:2011 Edition 3 until you change it). Edition 4 was gazetted on 30 May 2025 but its text is only sold by SABS, so type its numbers in once you have checked them; they are saved with the project and the report names the edition.
- **House placing sheet: title block and zoning table:** the PDF and DXF now carry a title block (project, stand, owner, architect, drawing number, revision, date, taken from Project details and a new "Drawing no." field in Plan labels) and the zoning list (building lines, coverage, floor area ratio, height, parking) with OK / NOT MET in red. In the DXF they sit on a TITLE BLOCK layer.
- **House placing, neighbour shade on the plan:** the tick "Show their shade on the plan" draws red wedges round the house, one per 10 degrees of compass bearing, with the angle above the horizon, exactly what "Send to the energy tools" hands over (screen only).
- **Council submission pack carries the House placing plan:** the checklist has a new line "Site plan with services (house placing: sewer, water, electrical)", made from House placing as an A3 PDF and joined into the pack with the other sheets. Projects saved earlier get the new line too.
- **Light and ventilation, light on the whole opening:** a new tick "Light on the whole opening" counts daylight on the full window or door opening including its frame, as SANS 10400-O 4.2.1.1.3 words it. Off (as before) counts the glass only. The glazing limit of SANS 10400-XA still uses the glass, the report says which basis was used, and the choice is saved with the project.
- **Rational design, shade from a building next door, on or off:** the simulation window has a tick "A building next to this one throws shade on it": type the compass bearing it lies in, its distance, height and width facing the house, and its shade is taken off the sun's beam hour by hour. It works with the neighbours' skyline sent from House placing (both can be on together), and a second tick decides whether the reference building gets the same shade or only the design does. Untick them all for no shade. The result page says which was used.
- **House placing hands over to the energy tools:** "Send to the energy tools" (in the zoning box) passes the stand number, area, coverage, the compass bearing of the house's long side (from the sheet's north arrow, road side and turn) and the skyline of the neighbouring houses to the report and the rational design. The simulation window has a new tick "Shade from the neighbouring houses": the neighbours' eaves (height typed in House placing, 3.5 m to start with) hide the sun's beam in the hours it is below their skyline, in the design and in the reference building alike. The north angle of the IFC is left as it is.
- **House placing, zoning compliance panel:** type the limits from the zoning scheme (coverage, floor area ratio, storeys, height, parking bays; 0 = none) and a tick / cross list under the stand sizes shows the three building lines, inside-the-stand, coverage, floor area ratio, height and parking, updating while you drag the house.
- **House placing, service checks:** the sewer fall is checked against SANS 10252-2 table 13 (flatter than 1:60 needs the municipality's special permission, flatter than 1:120 is not allowed, steeper than 1:10 needs anchor blocks); water, sewer and electrical lines that run out over a neighbouring stand now get a red ring on the plan and a warning. Cover (300 mm) and the water / sewer / electrical clearances were already checked.
- **House placing, DXF layers:** the export now puts the stand boundary (BOUNDARY), neighbours (NEIGHBOURS), road (ROAD), 1.2 m walls (WALLS), dimensions (DIMENSIONS) and the stand numbers (STAND NUMBERS) on their own layers, next to SEWER, WATER, ELECTRICAL, HOUSE, CARPORT and SETTING OUT.
- **House placing, dimensions tidied:** a dimension whose figure lands on another text or dimension now slides out across its line until it is clear (dimensions you moved by hand stay where you put them). The main water, sewer and electrical labels now fit the turned export frame instead of its bounding box.
- **House placing, the export frame carries every stand number inside it:** the numbers of all stands within 150 m are now read from the township plan (before, only the stands touching yours), and any stand that lies in or reaches into the blue frame gets its number written inside it on the DXF and PDF, also when the frame is turned. A number whose stand sticks out of the frame is moved in to sit just inside the edge. Load the stand again once so an older project picks the numbers up.
- **Light and ventilation rules checked against SANS 10400-O:2011 (Edition 3):** ventilation now also has the minimum opening area, 0.2 m2 per room (0.1 m2 for category 1 rooms), the larger of that and 5% of the floor area. The report names the edition. SANS 10400-O Edition 4 was gazetted on 30 May 2025; its text is sold by SABS and was not available to check, so confirm it against the new edition.
- **House placing, the blue export frame is always square to the paper and can be turned:** it no longer lies at 45 degrees when the plan is turned. Drag its edges and corners to resize, the round ring above it to turn it (it snaps back to square within 2 degrees, Shift gives steps of 15 degrees; the angle is shown), and the cross in its middle to move it. The DXF and PDF are cut to the frame as turned, and "Reset frame" puts it back square. Projects saved with the old frame open with a square frame that holds it.
- **Rational design: what to change to pass, and the certification roadmap:** when the design uses more than the reference building, the window now tries 11 changes (hot water system, ceiling and wall insulation, glass, shading, smaller east and west windows, lighting, air tightness, heating efficiency), shows what each saves on its own, and lists the combination that gets the design to or under the reference. The "How to get this certified" button sets out the Agrement South Africa protocol steps and where the built-in model stands against them.
- **Rational design brought in line with SANS 10400-XA:2021:** the reference building keeps your glass area and takes the table 4 U-value and SHGC for each storey's glass share (clear glass up to 20%); people at 75 W (two per bedroom unless you give the population); lights at table 12 with the table B.2 hours for dwellings; no appliance gain except offices and large shops; hot water per table 10 at 60 C (design system chosen in the window, the reference has half of it from a heat pump); demand is the monthly highest hour averaged over the year. The report now uses the 2021 clause numbers (4.2, 4.3, 4.4) and says plainly that clause 4.3 needs software certified by Agrement South Africa, which this built-in model is not.
- **Rational design from the left bar, with the site's weather:** a "Rational design" button under Energy (XA) in the left bar runs the simulation. It uses the weather file the project has (the one from "Get weather data for the site online"); when there is none it downloads the site's typical year the same way, keeps it, and uses it. If the email names an estate that has no square yet (for example *Albertsdal Ext 46*, also *Uitbreiding*, *Phase*, *Fase*), the program makes the square and puts the order in it; later extensions of that estate go in the same square. Extra email words you give an area (+ New area, Edit) work as before.
- **1.86.33** - The Orders tool now opens to large squares, one per area or development (for example Blue Hills). Click a square to see its houses, then a house to see its order lines. Add your own with **+ New area** (name and the words the client uses in emails); right-click a square to edit or delete it. A new order is put in the area named in the Township column of the email, the area that house already sits in, or the area whose name or words appear in the subject; otherwise it goes to **Not sorted**, and you can move a house with right-click > Move to another area. Urgent houses put their area at the top.
- **Orders is its own tool, 1.86.32**: the orders and the client changes have moved out of the Drawing register into a tool of their own on the start screen (a new Orders square), with their own left bar (Orders, Client changes) and their own ribbon: Watch inbox and Check now for Gmail, New order and Delete all, From drawings, New change and Delete all for client changes, and Attach, Open and Remove line for the client's email. The Drawing register keeps Drawings and Sent and revisions. Nothing is copied: the orders are still kept with the register, so they still go into the register email and PDF, and the inbox watcher keeps running whichever tool you are in. The urgent pop-up opens the Orders tool.
- **Priority row, calmer squares and urgent emails that name only a number, 1.86.31**: on the Orders squares, houses with an urgent order now sit in their own Priority row at the top (red heading), with the rest under All houses and blocks. Only urgent squares have the red border and tint now; a house with new orders keeps just its red N new line. An urgent email that names the house only by its number (for example 2037 is urgent) is matched to Stand 2037 from your register or your existing orders instead of ending up under Not sure which house; if it names two houses, each is marked.
- **Afrikaans emails, 1.86.30**: the inbox watcher now also recognises Afrikaans: urgent words (dringend, dringende, dadelik, onmiddellik, spoedeisend, spoed, so gou as moontlik, prioriteit, noodgeval), stand words (standplaas, huis, eenheid, perseel, blok; Standplaas 2037 in an email is matched to Stand 2037 in your register), Ekstras lines, finale planne, bestelling and opdrag numbers, and Groete / Dankie endings. The subject words for new setups are order, orders, bestelling, bestellings (add them to the box in Watch my Gmail inbox if you set your own words earlier).
- **Urgent orders and asked-again reminders, 1.86.29**: when the client writes about a stand that already has an order and the email says urgent (urgent, asap, immediately, priority, dringend, dadelik...), a pop-up appears with an Open the first one button, the house's square moves to the front with a red URGENT mark, and its order line moves to the top, tinted red and marked Urgent in the new Priority column. If the client only sends the same order again, no copy is made, the line is marked Asked again and the square says so. A new urgent order is flagged the same way. You can also set a line to Urgent or Asked again yourself in the Priority column; it drops back to normal when the order is sent. The priority is printed with the order in the email and the register PDF.
- **Delete all, and each order only once, 1.86.28**: the Orders and Client changes squares have a Delete all button (and right-click a square to delete just that house's lines). Deleting all orders also clears what the inbox watcher has read, so Check now brings the emails in again from the start. An order is now remembered by its house, wording and extras, not by the email it came in: the same order arriving in several emails (a forward, a resend, a longer list that repeats earlier stands) is added once, and copies already in the list are removed. A different order for the same house (other size or extras) is still added.
- **Extras on every order, 1.86.27**: each order now has its own Extras the client wants column next to What was ordered or changed, with a drop-down heading (click it to fold the list away or open it again). The extras read from the client's email (built in braai, enclose patio, additional patio, IBR flat roof and so on) are listed one per line, you can edit or add lines, and they are printed with the order in the email and the register PDF. Orders that already had an Extras line inside their text are moved into the new column.
- **No duplicate orders from the inbox, 1.86.26**: an email is now only ever turned into orders once (by its Gmail message number), an order that is already in the list with the same house, wording and email is not added again, the inbox watcher can no longer be started twice, and copies that are already in your list are removed (unsent lines only) the next time the program looks at the inbox.
- **Allow Gmail reading only once, 1.86.25**: signing in with Google again for sending (or after a restart) no longer drops the permission to read your inbox: the read permission is asked for again automatically in every later sign-in, and the program now checks what Google really allowed each time it refreshes its access instead of relying on a saved note. You only press Allow Gmail reading once. (While the Google project is in testing mode, Google itself ends every sign-in after 7 days; that needs the project to be published in Google Cloud Console to stop.)
- **Fix: the Gmail watcher could not read emails, 1.86.24**: the installed program does not contain Python's html.parser, which the table reader used ("No module named html.parser"). The tables are now read without it. The release check also compares the exact modules each release imports against the last full installer, so a code update can no longer need a part the installer lacks.
- **Check now and a 10 second inbox watch, 1.86.23**: the Orders squares have a Check now button that looks in your Gmail straight away (it works even when watching is off, and brings in the matching emails of the last 14 days that are not in yet), and the watcher now looks every 10 seconds (the shortest you can set; earlier settings of 30 or 60 seconds become 10). The line next to the buttons says when it last looked and whether anything was new.
- **Orders written as a stand number and a line, 1.86.22**: the Gmail inbox watcher now also reads the format where each stand number is on a line of its own, followed by what is wanted (for example 3339, then 91m2 (4+2) Extras: Patio with Extended Roof Structure). Each number becomes its own order; if the email says finals, masters or SDP, the matching kind of change is ticked on every order.
- **One email, many orders, 1.86.21**: when an order email holds a table (one row per stand, with columns such as Stand Number, Plan Size, Plan Configuration and YES/NO extras), the Gmail inbox watcher now makes one order for every row: the stand becomes the house, the order text lists the plan, sizes and the extras marked YES. A table with eight stands gives eight orders. Emails without a table are still split by the lines that name a stand, erf or house. The default subject words are now order and orders.
- **Houses and blocks as squares, and Gmail inbox watching, 1.86.20**: the Orders and Client changes tabs now open on a grid of squares, one per house or block, showing how many orders or changes it has and a red New count; click a square to open just its lines, and press the back button to return to the squares (more houses simply add more squares, with a scroll bar). On Orders, the new Watch my Gmail inbox for orders button looks at your Gmail every minute (you choose the subject words and the interval); an email that matches is saved as an .eml, split into one order per house (a line naming a stand, erf, house, unit, plot or block starts a new order, and your own house names are recognised), and added marked New with the client's email attached. Press Allow Gmail reading once (Google asks for read-only access to your mail; the program never deletes or changes mail). Emails already in your inbox are not imported unless you tick the last-14-days option.
